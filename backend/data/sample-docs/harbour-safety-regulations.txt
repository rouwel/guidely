Harbour Safety Regulations

These regulations apply to every vessel of more than ten tonnes that moors, berths,
or transits within the harbour limits, whether it is a passenger ferry, a fishing
boat, a merchantman, or a private yacht at anchor.

Claims. Any claim against the harbour authority - for loss, damage, injury, or
loss of cargo - must be filed in writing with the harbour office within fourteen
days of the incident that gave rise to it. Claims received after the fourteen-day
period are void, regardless of the merits of the case. This fourteen-day rule was
enacted in the immediate aftermath of the Neverdine disaster so that families and
cargo owners could not be left without an answer while the evidence was lost. The
claim should state the name of the vessel, the date and time, the nature of the
loss, and the amount sought.

Safety drills. Every passenger vessel shall run a minimum of four lifeboat and
abandonment drills per year, at least one of which must take place in the winter
months when the sound is at its roughest. Masters must keep a drill log, and the
log must be produced to a harbour inspector on demand. A vessel found not to have
kept its drill schedule may be detained until the drills are made up.

Debris. Any person who observes wreckage, casks, or floating cargo in the sound or
within the harbour limits shall report it immediately to the harbour office. Do
not attempt to recover debris yourself: the harbour tender is equipped to lift
cargo and is the only vessel authorised to do so. Debris is both a hazard to
navigation and, in some cases, evidence in an ongoing inquiry, so it must be
reported and marked, not salvaged and sold.

Pilotage. Vessels of more than five hundred tonnes must take an authorised pilot
for any berthing manoeuvre in the harbour. A master who refuses a pilot when
pilotage is compulsory shall bear full liability for any damage arising from the
berthing.

Mooring. Mooring fees accrue from the hour the vessel ties up at a public berth.
No vessel may be left unmanned alongside a public quay for more than forty-eight
hours without written permission of the harbour office, which may move the vessel
at the owner's expense after that period.

The harbour office is the sole authority for interpreting these regulations.
Disputes are heard by the Harbour Court, whose decisions are final.
