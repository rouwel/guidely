Guild Membership Rules

Membership of the Shipwrights' and Mariners' Guild is open to any citizen of the
harbour towns who works a trade of the sea. The order of the guild is: apprentice,
journeyman, master. A member enters as an apprentice at the spring festival, is
advanced to journeyman at the completion of his or her third year, and may seek
the rank of master after a further five years of verified work and the completion
of a master's task set by the wardens.

Dues. Every member pays an annual due, fixed by the guild council and payable at
the spring festival. A member who has not paid by the end of the festival season
is given a grace period of sixty days. If the dues remain unpaid after the grace
period the member is suspended: the suspended member may not practice the trade in
the harbour towns, may not use the guild hall, and loses the vote in guild
matters. Suspension is lifted on payment of the arrears plus a reinstatement fee
of one-tenth of the annual due. A member suspended for two consecutive years is
struck from the roll entirely and must reapply from the start of the apprentice
rank.

Apprenticeship. No apprentice may be indentured to a master of the same household.
The warden of apprentices keeps the indenture book and records every transfer.
An apprentice who is beaten, starved, or misused may appeal to the wardens, who
shall hear the matter within a fortnight and, if the complaint is found, may
dissolve the indenture and reassign the apprentice without loss of standing.

Journeymen. Journeymen may travel and practice where they find work, but must keep
their dues current in their home town and may not open a year-round shop of their
own. The first claim on a journeyman's work in any season belongs to the guild
master of the harbour town where the journeyman is resident.

Master's task. The master's task is set individually by a committee of three
masters, chosen by lot, and must be completed without the aid of apprentices. The
task is assessed by the same committee, and the granting of the master's rank is
proclaimed at the next festival.

Disputes. All disputes between members, or between a member and the guild council,
are heard by the wardens. The wardens' decisions are binding and final; the guild
does not recognise the harbour court for internal matters.
